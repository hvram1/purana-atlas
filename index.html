Purāṇa Atlas

Two Sanskrit texts, each verse tied to the second it is chanted in a recorded upanyāsam. Click a verse, hear it. Five more are in hand.

They are built to argue for two things the Purāṇas still lack: recitation that is addressable at the verse, and an index of names across the corpus. Read the two proposals →

How the timings were made

Nobody typed these timestamps. Each recording was transcribed, the transcript matched against the printed text, and every matched verse then force-aligned to the waveform — so an onset is a measurement of the audio, not an editorial guess. Where a verse is not marked, that is a claim too: the speaker did not chant it, or the pass could not find it. The atlas distinguishes those cases rather than smoothing them into one.

Reading the lanes

A lane is one reciter's traversal of the text. The Kārtika atlas has two, and they barely overlap: Speaker 1 reads the book straight through, while Speaker 2 follows the calendar, taking each day's verses from wherever the text supports them. Together they reach more of the work than either alone, and the verses both chant are the same words in two voices.

What you need to use it

Playback is a YouTube embed, so the page needs the network and a browser that will run youtube.com/iframe_api. Everything else — the text, the divisions, the measured timings — is served from this site and works offline once loaded.

The Sanskrit is set in Noto Serif Devanagari, which the site serves itself — you do not need a Devanagari font installed, and the page asks no third party for one.

What is coming

Five more recordings are in hand. None has been through the alignment pass yet, so nothing below is an atlas — each is a recording and a printed text that have not been put against each other. They are listed because the method above is what they are waiting for, not because a date has been set.

  • Varāha, Viṣṇu and Garuḍa Purāṇas — whole Purāṇas rather than a single māhātmya, so each will divide by aṃśa or kāṇḍa the way Virāṭa divides by upaparva, and each will reach a smaller share of its text than a month's māhātmya does. Expounded by Musiri Yagnarama Somayaji Dīkṣitar
  • Vaiśākha Māsa Māhātmya — Skanda Purāṇa, and the near twin of the Kārtika atlas already here: a month's māhātmya of comparable length, in the same corpus. Expounded by both reciters, so it should carry two lanes as Kārtika does. Musiri Yagnarama Somayaji Dīkṣitar · Pondicherry Vaisampayanar
  • Māgha Māsa Māhātmya — Padma Purāṇa, not Skanda, and a run of chapters inside a far larger khaṇḍa rather than a section of its own. That is a shape this site does not yet have. Also expounded by both. Musiri Yagnarama Somayaji Dīkṣitar · Pondicherry Vaisampayanar

A Tulā Kāverī māhātmya is not on that list, and the reason is worth stating rather than leaving as a silence: the verses it turns on already stand in the Kārtika atlas, at adhyāya 4. It may prove to be a reading of what is published here rather than a text of its own.

Why this exists

These two atlases are the working proof behind two proposals. They share a corpus, and each is worth more with the other: one would make the Purāṇas audible at the verse, the other would make them searchable by name.

Working note · not a proposal

How the index gets made

Both proposals ask for scholarly reading time. This note describes the instrument that reading time is spent inside — why a published page is derived rather than edited, and why an index of this kind cannot be a wiki. It turns on two passages in the Kārtika atlas: Satyabhāmā asking Kṛṣṇa what she did in a former birth, and a brahmin promised he will be reborn as Daśaratha. Two figures everyone knows, in two stories almost nobody does — and the corpus names both in its own chapter headings.
