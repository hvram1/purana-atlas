Purāṇa Atlas

Two Sanskrit texts, each verse tied to the second it is chanted in a recorded upanyāsam. Click a verse, hear it.

How the timings were made

Nobody typed these timestamps. Each recording was transcribed, the transcript matched against the printed text, and every matched verse then force-aligned to the waveform — so an onset is a measurement of the audio, not an editorial guess. Where a verse is not marked, that is a claim too: the speaker did not chant it, or the pass could not find it. The atlas distinguishes those cases rather than smoothing them into one.

Reading the lanes

A lane is one reciter's traversal of the text. The Kārtika atlas has two, and they barely overlap: Speaker 1 reads the book straight through, while Speaker 2 follows the calendar, taking each day's verses from wherever the text supports them. Together they reach more of the work than either alone, and the verses both chant are the same words in two voices.

What you need to use it

Playback is a YouTube embed, so the page needs the network and a browser that will run youtube.com/iframe_api. Everything else — the text, the divisions, the measured timings — is served from this site and works offline once loaded.

The Sanskrit is set in Noto Serif Devanagari if your system has it. Without a Devanagari font installed you will see boxes where the verses should be.

Why this exists

These two atlases are the working proof behind two proposals. They share a corpus, and each is worth more with the other: one would make the Purāṇas audible at the verse, the other would make them searchable by name.
